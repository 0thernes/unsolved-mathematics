# Errata & Post-Release Correction Policy

This file maintains the public, append-only errata and correction ledger for **The Unsolved Mathematics Atlas** (`0thernes/unsolved-mathematics`), its Lean 4.35.0 formal core (`lean/`), and its companion white paper (`paper/Donahue_2026_unsolved_mathematics_atlas.pdf`).

## Correction & Withdrawal Policy

1. **Errors found after a tagged release are NEVER silently rewritten.** Every factual, mathematical, citation, formalization, or metadata error discovered after publication is logged here with date, affected version/tag, finding class (`bug`, `error`, `issue`, `problem`, `concern`, `dilemma`, `conflict`, `gap`, `hole`, `blindspot`), root cause, and commit SHA of the fix.
2. **Formal proof defect protocol:** If any theorem in `lean/` is discovered to have a statement-fidelity mismatch, vacuous hypothesis, disallowed axiom, or unsound modeling assumption, it is immediately flagged in this ledger and in `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md` before or alongside a corrective tag.
3. **Withdrawal / Retraction protocol:** If a major headline claim or white paper is found to be fatally flawed, we publish an explicit withdrawal notice at the top of this file, in `README.md`, and on the affected GitHub Release / Zenodo record rather than deleting history.
4. **How to report an error:** Open a GitHub Issue using the Bug / Error / Factual Correction form or email `0_0@0thernes.art` (see [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) and [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md)).

## 2026-11-26 statement audit

An independent statement audit of `v0.5.0` (commit `0604df5`) was carried out on 2026-11-26. Audit log: <https://github.com/0thernes/0thernes-research-archive/blob/main/docs/reviews/2026-11-26-statement-audit.md>. Claim documents (`README.md`, `CLAIMS.md`, `LIMITATIONS.md`, `docs/`) and the descriptive text of `lean/formalization.yaml` were corrected in commit `22ace26` (and timestamps refreshed in `e56ac6e`). All six remaining items below — originally logged as open in `22ace26` — are **resolved in `v0.7.0`**, while the 33 declarations added in `v0.6.0`–`v0.7.0` have not yet undergone a separate independent statement audit.

### White paper (`paper/Donahue_2026_unsolved_mathematics_atlas.tex` and the `v0.6.0` PDF)

1. **Where:** `v0.6.0` abstract ("the $3n-1$ barrier"), Explicit Non-Claim box ("or methodological no-go barrier"), Section "Domain I" title ("$3n-1$ Sibling Barrier"), Theorem item ("$\varepsilon$-Invariance barrier").
   **What was wrong:** the wording presented a formal barrier. `thm_collatz_epsilon_invariance_barrier` states only that the $3n-1$ map $T_-$ has the Terras parity-word bijection for every $d$ and positive supercritical cycles at $5$ (period 3) and $17$ (period 11). No Lean statement quantifies over arguments or proofs.
   **Correction:** read those passages as "the $3n-1$ sibling comparison". The inference that arguments which do not distinguish $\varepsilon = \pm 1$ cannot settle $3n+1$ is a heuristic, not a formalized theorem.
   **Affects a theorem statement:** no. **Status in `v0.7.0`:** **RESOLVED** in the `v0.7.0` white paper, `README.md`, `CLAIMS.md`, `LIMITATIONS.md`, `docs/faq.md`, `docs/glossary.md`, `docs/CROSS_ATLAS_AUDIT_2026-11-25.md`, `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md`, and `lean/formalization.yaml`.

2. **Where:** `v0.6.0` Explicit Non-Claim box ("Lonely Runner for $n \ge 4$ and uniform $n$ ... remain open") and the Lonely Runner theorem block.
   **What was wrong:** the state of the art was misstated and prior work was not credited. The Lonely Runner conjecture is proved for up to 13 runners in recent preprints: Rosenfeld, `arXiv:2509.14111` (8 runners); Trakulthongchai, `arXiv:2511.22427` (9 and 10 runners); Sungkawichai and Trakulthongchai, `arXiv:2604.23906` (11 to 13 runners). The 4-runner case formalized as `thm_lonely_runner_n3` is classical (Betke–Wills 1972; Cusick 1973).
   **Correction:** "the Lonely Runner conjecture remains open in general; it is proved for up to 13 runners (preprints above). The cases $n \le 3$ formalized here are classical."
   **Affects a theorem statement:** no. **Status in `v0.7.0`:** **RESOLVED** in the `v0.7.0` white paper (with all three live-verified arXiv references added to `paper/references.bib`) and all repository docs.

### `lean/` documentation

3. **Where:** `v0.6.0` `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md` (`thm_lonely_runner_n3_nondiv4`, "Elementary mod-4 coverage (7/8 classes)"); also the historical `CHANGELOG.md` entry for `[0.5.0]` ("all `7/8` mod-4 residue combinations").
   **What was wrong:** the hypothesis is $4 \nmid v_1, v_2, v_3$. Of the $4^3 = 64$ residue classes of speed triples mod 4, $3^3 = 27$ satisfy it. No reading gives 7/8. The proof uses the single witness $t = 1/4$.
   **Correction:** "27 of 64 speed-triple residue classes mod 4 (no speed divisible by 4), witness $t = 1/4$".
   **Affects a theorem statement:** no. **Status in `v0.7.0`:** **RESOLVED** in `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md`, `README.md`, `CLAIMS.md`, `LIMITATIONS.md`, `docs/CROSS_ATLAS_AUDIT_2026-11-25.md`, and the `v0.7.0` white paper (historical `[0.5.0]` changelog text preserved as record).

4. **Where:** `v0.6.0` `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md` ("$n \ge 4$ and the general uniform conjecture for all $n$ remain OPEN").
   **What was wrong:** $n = 4$ to $12$ (5 to 13 runners) are proved in the preprints cited in item 2.
   **Correction:** "not formalized here for $n \ge 4$; proved in the literature for up to 13 runners; open in general".
   **Affects a theorem statement:** no. **Status in `v0.7.0`:** **RESOLVED** in `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md`.

### Formal code (`lean/`)

6. **Where:** `v0.6.0` `lean/FormalAtlas/Defs.lean` (`divSumPairs`, `divSum`, `IsPerfect`).
   **What was wrong:** `divSum n` sums divisor pairs up to `isqrt n`. `isqrt_spec` was proved in `v0.6.0`, but no Lean lemma stated that `divSum n` equals $\sum_{d \mid n} d$; agreement rested on pytest differential checks.
   **Correction:** prove an unconditional Lean lemma equating `divSum n` with the literal linear divisor sum $\sum_{d=1}^{n} [d \mid n]\, d$ and cite it in `STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md`.
   **Affects a theorem statement:** yes, in its reading. **Status in `v0.7.0`:** **RESOLVED** — `divSum_eq_divSumLin` in `lean/FormalAtlas/PerfectNumbers.lean` now proves `divSum n = divSumLin n n` unconditionally for ALL $n \in \mathbb{N}$ in Lean 4.35.0 (used directly to prove `thm_euler_even_perfect_converse` and `thm_euclid_euler_even_perfect_iff`) and is cited in `lean/STATEMENT_FIDELITY.md`.
