# Top Mathematicians — The Riesel Problem

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Name | Era | Role | Contribution |
|---|------|-----|------|--------------|
| 1 | Hans Riesel | 1956–2014 | originator | Discovered $k = 509{,}203$ (1956), posed the problem, and developed the LLR primality test. |
| 2 | Paul Erdős | 1950–1996 | precursor | Invented covering systems of congruences (1950) and posed the minimum-modulus problem. |
| 3 | Wacław Sierpiński | 1960 | precursor | Proved the sibling $k \cdot 2^n + 1$ theorem (1960). |
| 4 | John Selfridge | 1962 | breakthrough | Found the conjectured smallest Sierpiński number $78{,}557$ (1962). |
| 5 | Michael Filaseta | 1990s–present | breakthrough | Deep structural theorems on covering systems, Sierpiński, and Riesel numbers. |
| 6 | Wilfrid Keller | 1980s–present | computational | Pioneer of computational Riesel/Sierpiński prime-elimination tables. |
| 7 | Yves Gallot | 2000s–present | computational | Authored `Proth.exe` and `PRST`, accelerating distributed LLR testing. |
| 8 | Jean Penné | 2000s–present | computational | Authored the `LLR` Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel software powering PrimeGrid. |
| 9 | Bob Hough | 2015–present | breakthrough | Proved the minimum-modulus bound for covering systems (*Annals*, 2015). |
| 10 | PrimeGrid Distributed Computing Community | 2010–present | frontier | Eliminated hundreds of stubborn $k < 509{,}203$ candidates via multi-million-digit primes. |
