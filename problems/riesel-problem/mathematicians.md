# Top Mathematicians — The Riesel Problem

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Mathematician | Contribution |
|---|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | Hans Riesel | Discovered $k = 509{,}203$ (1956), posed the problem, and developed the LLR primality test |
| 2 | Paul Erdős | Invented covering systems of congruences (1950) and posed the minimum-modulus problem |
| 3 | Wacław Sierpiński | Proved the sibling $k \cdot 2^n + 1$ theorem (1960) |
| 4 | John Selfridge | Found the conjectured smallest Sierpiński number $78{,}557$ (1962) |
| 5 | Michael Filaseta | Deep structural theorems on covering systems, Sierpiński, and Riesel numbers |
| 6 | Wilfrid Keller | Pioneer of computational Riesel/Sierpiński prime-elimination tables |
| 7 | Yves Gallot | Authored `Proth.exe` and `PRST`, accelerating distributed LLR testing |
| 8 | Jean Penné | Authored the `LLR` Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel software powering PrimeGrid |
| 9 | Bob Hough | Proved the minimum-modulus bound for covering systems (`Annals` 2015) |
| 10 | PrimeGrid Distributed Computing Community | Eliminated hundreds of stubborn $k < 509{,}203$ candidates via multi-million-digit primes |
