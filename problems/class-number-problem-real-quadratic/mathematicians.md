# Top Mathematicians — Gauss's Class Number Problem for Real Quadratic Fields

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Name | Era | Role | Contribution |
|---|------|-----|------|--------------|
| 1 | Carl Friedrich Gauss | 1801 | originator | Formulated both imaginary and real class number problems (1801). |
| 2 | Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet | 1839 | breakthrough | Proved the analytic class number formula linking $h(D)$, $\varepsilon_D$, and $L(1, \chi_D)$ (1839). |
| 3 | Carl Ludwig Siegel | 1935 | breakthrough | Proved the asymptotic $h(D) \log \varepsilon_D = D^{1/2 + o(1)}$ (1935). |
| 4 | Kurt Heegner | 1952 | breakthrough | Solved the imaginary $h=1$ problem via modular functions (1952). |
| 5 | Alan Baker & Harold Stark | 1966–1967 | breakthrough | Independently settled the imaginary $h=1$ problem (1966–1967). |
| 6 | Dorian Goldfeld | 1976–1985 | breakthrough | Linked effective imaginary class number bounds to rank-3 elliptic curves (1976, 1985). |
| 7 | Benedict Gross & Don Zagier | 1986 | breakthrough | Proved the height formula completing Goldfeld's effective imaginary solution (1986). |
| 8 | Harold Davenport & Hans Heilbronn | 1971 | breakthrough | Computed the exact average 3-torsion in quadratic class groups (1971). |
| 9 | Henri Cohen & Hendrik Lenstra | 1984 | frontier | Predicted the exact density $\approx 75.446\%$ of $h(p)=1$ real quadratic fields (1984). |
| 10 | Manjul Bhargava | 2004–present | frontier | Extended arithmetic-invariant counting of class groups and discriminants to higher degrees. |
