# Originator(s) — The Erdős–Turán Conjecture on Additive Bases

_Biography, background, and the ideas that led here._

**Paul Erdős (1913–1996)** and **Pál Turán (1910–1976)** were lifelong collaborators and central pillars of the Hungarian school of combinatorics and analytic number theory. Their 1941 paper in the *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* launched modern additive combinatorics by connecting Simon Sidon's Fourier-series questions (bounds on sets with distinct pairwise sums) directly to the global behavior of representation functions $r_A(n)$ for bases.

Turán's analytic intuition and Erdős's extremal/probabilistic instincts combined to ask whether the positivity constraint $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ forces unavoidable "pile-ups" in the convolution $1_A * 1_A$ whenever $1_A * 1_A$ is bounded away from zero at infinity.
