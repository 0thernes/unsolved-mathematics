# Top Mathematicians — The Happy Ending Problem (Erdős–Szekeres Conjecture)

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Mathematician | Contribution |
|---|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | Esther Klein (Szekeres) | Proved $\mathrm{ES}(4) = 5$ and originated the problem in 1933 |
| 2 | Paul Erdős | Co-proved finiteness (1935), the lower bound $2^{n-2}+1$ (1961), and set the \$500 prize |
| 3 | George Szekeres | Co-proved the 1935/1961 theorems and co-settled $\mathrm{ES}(6) = 17$ (2006) |
| 4 | Imre Bárány & Pavel Valtr | Proved the positive-fraction Erdős–Szekeres theorem (1998) |
| 5 | Géza Tóth | Proved sharp binomial upper bounds and geometric Ramsey refinements (1998–2005) |
| 6 | Attila Pór | Co-proved the strengthened positive-fraction theorem key to Suk's argument |
| 7 | Walter Morris & Valeriu Soltan | Authored the authoritative *Bull. AMS* survey (2000) |
| 8 | Andrew Suk | Shattered the $4^n$ barrier with $\mathrm{ES}(n) \le 2^{n + O(n^{2/3}\log n)}$ (2016/2017) |
| 9 | Andreas Holmsen & János Pach | Co-proved the current record $2^{n + O(\sqrt{n \log n})}$ (2020) |
| 10 | Gábor Tardos | Co-proved the 2020 *JEMS* refinement extending to pseudoline arrangements |
