# Top Mathematicians — The Černý Conjecture

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Mathematician | Contribution |
|---|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | Ján Černý | Constructed the extremal $(n-1)^2$ family and posed the conjecture (1964) |
| 2 | Jean-Éric Pin | Reduced the upper bound to extremal set theory and proved the $n^3/6$ bound with Frankl (1983) |
| 3 | Peter Frankl | Proved the extremal two-family set theorem underpinning the 35-year $n^3/6$ bound (1982) |
| 4 | Jarkko Kari | Proved the conjecture for Eulerian automata via linear algebra and disproved Pin's stronger variant |
| 5 | Mikhail V. Volkov | Leading authority on synchronizing automata and author of the canonical surveys (2008, 2022) |
| 6 | Avraham Trahtman | Proved the Road Coloring Theorem (2009) and the Černý conjecture for aperiodic automata |
| 7 | Benjamin Steinberg | Applied representation theory to prove quadratic bounds for one-cluster & prime-state automata |
| 8 | Marek Szykuła | First broke the 35-year Pin–Frankl $n^3/6$ bound ($0.16642\,n^3$, 2017/2018) |
| 9 | Yaroslav Shitov | Proved the current world-record upper bound $0.1654\,n^3 + o(n^3)$ (2019) |
| 10 | Cyril Nicaud | Proved that random synchronizing automata have expected reset length $O(n^{1/2 + \varepsilon})$ |
