# Top Mathematicians — The Černý Conjecture

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Name | Era | Role | Contribution |
|---|------|-----|------|--------------|
| 1 | Ján Černý | 1964 | originator | Constructed the extremal $(n-1)^2$ family and posed the conjecture (1964). |
| 2 | Jean-Éric Pin | 1978–1983 | breakthrough | Reduced the upper bound to extremal set theory and proved the $n^3/6$ bound with Frankl (1983). |
| 3 | Peter Frankl | 1982 | breakthrough | Proved the extremal two-family set theorem underpinning the 35-year $n^3/6$ bound (1982). |
| 4 | Jarkko Kari | 2003–present | breakthrough | Proved the conjecture for Eulerian automata via linear algebra and disproved Pin's stronger variant. |
| 5 | Mikhail V. Volkov | 2008–present | survey | Leading authority on synchronizing automata and author of the canonical surveys (2008, 2022). |
| 6 | Avraham Trahtman | 2007–2009 | breakthrough | Proved the Road Coloring Theorem (2009) and the Černý conjecture for aperiodic automata. |
| 7 | Benjamin Steinberg | 2010–present | breakthrough | Applied representation theory to prove quadratic bounds for one-cluster & prime-state automata. |
| 8 | Marek Szykuła | 2017–present | frontier | First broke the 35-year Pin–Frankl $n^3/6$ bound ($0.16642\,n^3$, 2017/2018). |
| 9 | Yaroslav Shitov | 2019–present | frontier | Proved the current world-record upper bound $0.1654\,n^3 + o(n^3)$ (2019). |
| 10 | Cyril Nicaud | 2016–present | frontier | Proved that random synchronizing automata have expected reset length $O(n^{1/2 + \varepsilon})$. |
