# Top Mathematicians — The Bateman–Horn Conjecture

_Ten figures who shaped the problem, past and present._

| # | Name | Era | Role | Contribution |
|---|------|-----|------|--------------|
| 1 | Paul T. Bateman | 1962 | originator | Co-formulated the quantitative asymptotic conjecture and Euler product (1962). |
| 2 | Roger A. Horn | 1962 | originator | Co-authored the foundational 1962 paper and numerical verification. |
| 3 | Viktor Bunyakovsky | 1857 | precursor | Posed the single-polynomial qualitative precursor (1857). |
| 4 | G. H. Hardy & J. E. Littlewood | 1923 | precursor | Formulated the linear $k$-tuple and quadratic prime asymptotics (1923). |
| 5 | Andrzej Schinzel | 1958 | precursor | Formulated qualitative Hypothesis H for polynomial systems (1958). |
| 6 | Atle Selberg | 1940s–1950s | breakthrough | Identified the sieve parity barrier blocking direct combinatorial proofs. |
| 7 | Henryk Iwaniec | 1978–1998 | breakthrough | Proved $n^2+1$ takes infinitely many $P_2$ values (1978) and co-proved $x^2+y^4$ primes (1998). |
| 8 | John Friedlander | 1998 | breakthrough | Co-proved the $x^2 + y^4$ prime asymptotic theorem (1998). |
| 9 | James Maynard | 2013–present | frontier | Proved bounded gaps between primes and primes represented by incomplete norm forms. |
| 10 | Will Sawin & Mark Shusterman | 2019–present | frontier | Proved function-field analogues of Chowla and Bateman–Horn over $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$. |
